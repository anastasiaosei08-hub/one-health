One Health is a global approach that recognises the health of humans, animals, and our environment are deeply linked. When one is sick, the others suffer too. Together, we can protect all three.

Human Health

People stay healthy when the animals around them are healthy and the air, water, and land are clean. Many diseases — like flu, COVID-19, and Ebola — started in animals and spread to humans. Doctors and community health workers must work together with vets and environmentalists.

🐄

Animal Health

Animals — both wild and domestic — carry diseases that can move to humans. When livestock are sick, families lose food and income. Vaccinating animals, stopping illegal wildlife trade, and keeping farm conditions clean protects both animals and the people who live near them.

🌿

Environment

Forests, rivers, soil, and air are the foundation of all life. When we destroy forests, pollute water, or change the climate, wild animals move closer to people — and diseases spread more easily. A healthy planet keeps humans and animals healthy too.

Real examples

How They Connect in Real Life

🦇

Ebola

Ebola virus lives in bats. When people hunt or eat infected animals in forests, the disease spreads to humans. Protecting forests reduced outbreaks.

🐓

Bird Flu

Bird flu spreads from chickens to people at live animal markets. Good hygiene, animal checks, and quick reporting stops it spreading further.

💧

Dirty Water

When animal waste enters rivers, people who drink or bathe in that water get sick. Clean water protects communities, animals, and ecosystems together.

💊

Antibiotic Resistance

When farmers overuse antibiotics in animals, bacteria become resistant. Those same resistant bacteria can then infect people and be very hard to treat.

What Can We Do?

Every person, every community can take simple steps to protect human, animal, and environmental health together.

🧼 Wash hands often
🐾 Vaccinate your animals
🌳 Do not cut forests
🗑️ Dispose of waste safely
📢 Report sick animals
🤝 Work across sectors
